The Bonus & Surplus Playbook

Irregular income is where the largest single decisions get made fastest and with the least thought. A bonus, a vesting parcel, a distribution, the month a loan finally clears. Decide the rule before the money arrives — because the decision made in the moment is almost never the one you would have chosen in advance.

Why a rule beats a decision. A bonus arrives alongside a reason to celebrate, a renovation quote and a car that is three years old. Every one of those is reasonable. A rule set in a calm month is not competing with any of them.

Step one · Start from the net, never the gross

A bonus is announced in gross and spent in net. At a 47% marginal rate including the Medicare levy, roughly half of the headline figure was never yours to allocate.

Four ways this goes wrong

Every one of these is something sensible people do.

Letting it sit "until I decide"

Money parked in a transaction account pending a decision is money being spent in instalments. The decision is usually made by attrition rather than by choice.

Treating the gross figure as the budget

Commitments get made against the announced number, and the net arrives months later roughly half the size. The shortfall is then funded by the surplus that was supposed to be invested.

Contributing to super without checking the cap

Concessional contributions include what your employer already pays. Exceeding the cap has tax consequences, and the carry-forward rules that might have helped depend on your total super balance. Check before, not after.

Allocating 100% to the sensible option

A rule with no enjoyment in it does not survive its second year. A deliberate small share for something you actually want is what makes the other 90% automatic.
