The Gap Years

If you stop at 55 and super unlocks at 60, five years need funding from somewhere else. This works out how much.

The rule that creates the gap. From 1 July 2024, preservation age is 60 for everyone who had not already reached it. Stop before 60 and every one of those years is funded from capital held outside superannuation.

Capital needed to bridge the gap —

The bridge figure is the simple sum of the net annual need across those years, in today's dollars. It makes no allowance for inflation, tax, the returns earned while the capital is being drawn down, or the order in which those returns arrive.

The years before 60 are the ones people picture first. Funding them is a separate exercise from funding retirement.

The three ways this gap closes

There are only so many levers. Most plans use some of each, and the mix is the decision — not the arithmetic above it.

Lever one

Build outside super

Direct more of the surplus into capital you can reach before 60. The trade-off is giving up some of super's concessional treatment on the way in.

Lever two

Move the date

Every year later shortens the bridge and lengthens the accumulation. It is the bluntest lever, and usually the one people reach for last.

Lever three

Change the shape of the years

Part-time work, consulting or a staged exit reduces the net annual need. A smaller need across fewer years is a far smaller number.

Take this to a conversation

The bridge figure is the question, not the answer. Which lever fits depends on your marginal tax rate, your existing structures, your debt and what you want those years to look like — and that is a conversation, not a calculator. Three worth bringing:

  • Which of my assets could I actually reach before 60 — and which only look like they could?
  • If the date moved by two years, what would that do to the size of the bridge?
  • Do the first years of this cost the same as the ones that follow them?
Important limitations. A simplified educational illustration. The bridge figure is an undiscounted sum in today's dollars and does not account for inflation, tax, returns earned during the drawdown, sequencing risk, the age pension, or any condition of release that may apply to you.
