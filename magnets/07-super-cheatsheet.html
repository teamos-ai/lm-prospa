The trap in the concessional cap

Thresholds that bite

The numbers most people meet after the fact rather than before it.

Division 293 threshold $250,000 income plus concessional contributions

Above it, concessional contributions are effectively taxed at 30% rather than 15%. That narrows the gap to your marginal rate; it does not usually close it.

Carry-forward concessional $500,000 total super balance, prior 30 June

Under it, unused cap from the previous five years is still available to you. Unused amounts expire after five years.

Superannuation guarantee 12% of ordinary time earnings

Unchanged for 2026–27, and counted inside the concessional cap.

What changed this year

One genuinely new thing on this page.

Division 296 — the tax on large balances — now applies. From 1 July 2026, an additional tax applies to superannuation earnings attributable to the part of a total super balance above $3 million, taking the effective rate on that portion to around 30%. A further tier applies above $10 million, taking the effective rate on that portion to around 40%. Both thresholds are indexed — the $3 million threshold in $150,000 steps and the $10 million threshold in $500,000 steps. Unrealised gains are excluded: the tax applies to realised earnings. The first balances measured are those at 30 June 2027.

This is new law with its first measurement date still ahead. If your balance is anywhere near $3 million, the detail of how earnings are attributed matters considerably more than the headline rate — confirm the current position before acting on it.

When you can get at it

Two numbers, and both are simpler than they used to be.

Preservation age 60 for everyone still working

From 1 July 2024, preservation age is 60 for everyone who had not already reached it. The old sliding scale by birth year no longer produces a different answer for anyone still working.

Tax on withdrawals after 60 Generally nil taxable component, taxed source

Once a condition of release is met. Untaxed elements, defined benefit schemes and death benefits follow different rules.

Most of these numbers index every year. The decisions they allow do not wait that long.

Eight shortcuts in these numbers

Each one is a decision already available to you. Mark the ones that apply — the list you build is the agenda, and it prints with the page.

Your agenda, as you build it

Marks are kept in this browser only. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Use five years of unused cap in one year TaxMoney

If your total super balance was under $500,000 at the last 30 June, cap you did not use in the previous five years is still available. A year with a bonus, a sale or a redundancy is exactly when that headroom is worth having.

The trade-off: the window closes the moment your balance passes $500,000, and unused amounts expire after five years. This is the shortcut with a deadline on it.
Fill the cap instead of guessing at it TaxTime

$32,500 less what your employer already pays is a figure your payroll team can give you in one email. Setting the arrangement once means never deciding again.

The trade-off: anything contributed is locked until 60, and exceeding the cap has tax consequences. Get the headroom figure first, not after.
Find out whether Division 293 applies to you KnowledgeMoney

Above $250,000 of combined income and concessional contributions, those contributions are effectively taxed at 30% rather than 15%. It narrows the advantage — it rarely removes it — but it changes the arithmetic you are deciding on.

The trade-off: none, beyond the ten minutes it takes to find out. Discovering it on an assessment afterwards is the expensive version.
Find the accounts you forgot you had MoneyAdmin

A career of employers leaves a trail of accounts, each charging fees and some carrying insurance you are paying for twice. Your myGov account lists them all.

The trade-off: consolidating can cancel insurance held in the closing account, and that cover may not be replaceable at your current age or health. Check the insurance before you close anything.
Look at the option you were defaulted into MoneyTime

A default option is a decision made by someone who has never met you, and it is set for an average member rather than for your timeframe. Over twenty years the difference compounds into real money.

The trade-off: more growth assets means more volatility, and the right answer depends on how long until you draw on it. This is a review, not an automatic switch.
Put a binding nomination in place — and diarise it RiskAdmin

Super is not automatically an estate asset. Without a valid binding nomination the trustee decides where it goes — and a lapsing nomination expires silently, usually after three years.

The trade-off: binding means binding, including if circumstances change and you forget to update it. That is the argument for the diary note, not against the nomination.
Decide where a bonus goes before it is earned TaxKnowledge

A salary sacrifice arrangement has to be in place before the income is earned. Asking in June about a bonus already paid in May is a conversation about what you could have done.

The trade-off: you commit before you know the amount, and anything sacrificed is locked until 60. Cap headroom has to be checked first.
Look at the CGT cap long before a business sale TaxMoney

Eligible small-business sale proceeds can go into super under a separate lifetime cap of $1,935,000, outside the non-concessional cap. For an owner, it is the largest single contribution opportunity that exists.

The trade-off: eligibility is strict and is decided by how the business is structured and held — years before the sale, not at settlement. Late is the same as ineligible.

Five questions to ask

If you cannot answer one of these, that is the one worth asking about.

01
How much concessional headroom do I actually have this year?

$32,500 less what your employer will contribute, less any salary sacrifice already in place.

02
Does Division 293 apply to me, and have I accounted for it?

It changes what a concessional contribution is worth — it rarely makes it worthless.

03
Was my total super balance under $500,000 at the last 30 June?

If so, up to five years of unused concessional cap may still be available. If not, the opportunity has closed.

04
If I want work to be optional before 60, what funds those years?

Nothing on this page does. That is a question for capital held outside super.

05
Who would receive this if I died tomorrow, and what would they pay?

Super is not automatically an estate asset, and the tax outcome depends on who receives it.
