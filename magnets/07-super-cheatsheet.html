The Executive Super Cheatsheet

The superannuation numbers that actually change decisions on a high income, for the 2026–27 Australian financial year. One page. Keep it until July, then check it again — most of these index.

Contribution caps

Annual, and largely use-it-or-lose-it.

Concessional cap $32,500

Before-tax: employer SG, salary sacrifice and personal deductible contributions, combined. Up from $30,000.

Non-concessional cap $130,000

After-tax contributions, where your total super balance allows them.

Bring-forward maximum $390,000

Up to three years of non-concessional cap brought into one year, subject to your balance.

The trap in the concessional cap. It includes what your employer already pays. On a salary at or above the maximum contribution base, employer contributions alone consume a large share of the $32,500 before you have salary sacrificed a dollar. Check the headroom before committing a bonus to super.

The thresholds that bite on a high income

These are the ones most often discovered after the fact.

What 2026–27 Why it matters to you
Division 293 threshold $250,000 Above this — income plus concessional contributions, combined — concessional contributions are effectively taxed at 30% rather than 15%. It narrows the gap to your marginal rate; it does not usually close it.
Carry-forward concessional TSB under $500,000 Unused concessional cap from the previous five years can be used, but only if your total super balance was under $500,000 on the prior 30 June. Unused amounts expire after five years.
Superannuation guarantee 12% Of ordinary time earnings. Unchanged for 2026–27.
Maximum contribution base $270,830 The quarterly-equivalent earnings ceiling above which an employer is not required to pay SG. Earnings beyond it generate no further compulsory contribution.
Transfer balance cap $2.1 million The lifetime limit on what can move into a tax-free retirement-phase income stream. Increased from $2 million on 1 July 2026.
CGT cap (lifetime) $1,935,000 For eligible small-business sale proceeds contributed to super, outside the non-concessional cap. Relevant if a business exit is part of your plan.

What changed on 1 July 2026

The one genuinely new thing on this page.

Division 296 — the tax on large balances — now applies. From 1 July 2026, an additional tax applies to superannuation earnings attributable to the part of a total super balance above $3 million, taking the effective rate on that portion to around 30%. A further tier applies above $10 million, taking the effective rate on that portion to around 40%. Both thresholds are indexed — the $3 million threshold in $150,000 steps and the $10 million threshold in $500,000 steps. Unrealised gains are excluded: the tax applies to realised earnings. The first balances measured are those at 30 June 2027.

This is new law with its first measurement date still ahead. If your balance is anywhere near $3 million, the detail of how earnings are attributed matters considerably more than the headline rate — confirm the current position before acting on it.

Access

Preservation age 60

From 1 July 2024, preservation age is 60 for everyone who had not already reached it. The old sliding scale by birth year no longer produces a different answer for anyone still working.

Tax on withdrawals after 60 Generally nil

On the taxable component of a benefit from a taxed source, once a condition of release is met. Untaxed elements, defined benefit schemes and death benefits follow different rules.

Five questions this page should prompt

If you cannot answer one of these, that is the one worth asking about.

How much concessional headroom do I actually have this year?

$32,500 less what your employer will contribute, less any salary sacrifice already in place.

Does Division 293 apply to me, and have I accounted for it?

It changes what a concessional contribution is worth — it rarely makes it worthless.

Was my total super balance under $500,000 at the last 30 June?

If so, up to five years of unused concessional cap may still be available. If not, the opportunity has closed.

If I want work to be optional before 60, what funds those years?

Nothing on this page does. That is a question for capital held outside super.

Who would receive this if I died tomorrow, and what would they pay?

Super is not automatically an estate asset, and the tax outcome depends on who receives it.
