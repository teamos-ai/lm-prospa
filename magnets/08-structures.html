Wealth Structures, Compared

Where an asset is held changes what it earns you after tax, when you can reach it, what happens when you sell it and where it goes when you die. Structure is decided once and lived with for decades — and unwinding it later can trigger the very tax it was meant to manage.

How to read this. No structure wins. Each one is good at something and costs you something else. The question is never "which is best" — it is "which trade-off suits my marginal rate, my timeframe, my family and my tolerance for complexity". This page is a map of the trade-offs, not a recommendation of any of them.

The six at a glance

Structure How income is taxed When you can reach it Commonly considered when The usual catch
Personal nameThe default At your marginal rate, so up to 47% with the Medicare levy. Any time. Simplicity matters, amounts are modest, or the asset produces little income. The most expensive place for income to land if you are a high earner.
Spouse or jointSplitting the rate At each owner's marginal rate, on their share. Any time. There is a genuine and durable difference between two marginal rates. Rates converge over time. Transferring later can trigger capital gains tax and duty.
Family trustDiscretionary Distributed each year to beneficiaries, who are taxed at their own rates. Any time, subject to the deed and the trustee. Income can usefully be directed among adult family members, and asset protection matters. Setup and annual costs, strict distribution rules, and losses are trapped inside the trust.
CompanyA flat rate At the company tax rate, with franking credits attaching to dividends paid out. Any time, but getting money out personally is a taxable event. Retaining and reinvesting profits, often alongside a business. No capital gains tax discount for companies. Getting funds out is where the tax arrives.
Investment bondTax paid inside Inside the bond at the company tax rate. No annual return obligation for you. Any time, though the tax treatment improves once held for ten years. A long horizon, a high marginal rate, and a preference for simplicity over flexibility. Contribution rules apply year to year, and withdrawing early changes the outcome.
SuperannuationThe concession Concessionally inside the fund, and generally tax-free on withdrawal after 60. Preservation age — 60 for everyone reaching it from 1 July 2024. The timeframe is genuinely beyond 60 and the gap to your marginal rate is wide. Locked. Annual caps. And from 1 July 2026, an extra tax on earnings above $3 million.

The trade-off in each

What you gain, what it costs, and the question that decides it.

Personal name

Simplest · Least efficient at high rates
Gains you
No setup cost, no annual compliance, complete control, and the 50% capital gains tax discount on assets held over twelve months.
Costs you
Every dollar of income is taxed at your rate. At 47%, an investment yielding 4% delivers barely over 2% after tax.
The deciding question
Is the income meaningful enough to justify the cost and complexity of holding it elsewhere?

Spouse or joint ownership

Simple · Depends on a rate difference lasting
Gains you
Income taxed where the rate is lower. No structure to establish, and nothing to administer each year.
Costs you
It stops working when the rates converge, and moving assets between names later is usually a capital gains tax event — sometimes a duty event too.
The deciding question
Is the difference between the two marginal rates both large and likely to persist?

Family trust

Flexible · Ongoing obligations
Gains you
Income can be directed each year among eligible beneficiaries, so the destination can change as circumstances do. A degree of asset protection, and the capital gains tax discount can flow through to individuals.
Costs you
Establishment and annual accounting costs, strict rules about valid distributions and their timing, close regulatory attention to distributions to adult children, and losses that cannot leave the trust.
The deciding question
Are there genuinely other adults whose rates make distributions worthwhile, year after year?

Company

Flat rate while retained · Taxed on the way out
Gains you
Profits retained and reinvested at the company rate rather than your marginal rate, with franking credits attaching to dividends when they are eventually paid.
Costs you
No capital gains tax discount at the company level, compliance obligations, and rules governing loans and payments to shareholders. The tax is deferred, not avoided.
The deciding question
Will the profits genuinely stay inside and be reinvested, or will you need them personally?

Investment bond

Low maintenance · Rules reward patience
Gains you
Tax is paid inside the bond, so there is no annual income to declare. Nothing to administer, no distributions to decide, and a defined beneficiary on death.
Costs you
Flexibility. Contributions are limited year to year, and withdrawing before the ten-year mark changes the treatment. Fees and the underlying investment menu matter.
The deciding question
Is the horizon genuinely ten years or more, and is your rate high enough for the internal rate to be an improvement?

Superannuation

Most concessional · Least accessible
Gains you
Concessional tax on contributions and earnings, and generally tax-free withdrawals after 60. The single most favourable treatment available to most people.
Costs you
Access. Annual caps limit how fast you can use it, Division 293 narrows the benefit above $250,000 of combined income, and from 1 July 2026 an additional tax applies to earnings on balances above $3 million.
The deciding question
Do you need any of this money before 60? If yes, the concession is irrelevant for that portion.

What usually decides it

In practice the answer is rarely one structure. It is a small number of them, each holding the assets it suits, sized against when you want the money. The common mistake is not choosing the wrong structure — it is choosing one, and then putting everything in it out of habit.

General information only. This is an educational comparison of how different ownership structures are generally treated in Australia. It is not a recommendation of any structure, it is not tax or legal advice, and it does not consider your objectives, financial situation or needs. Tax treatment, eligibility, establishment requirements and ongoing obligations vary with your circumstances and change over time. Structures carry legal, tax and administrative consequences that should be considered with a qualified adviser, accountant and, where relevant, a lawyer before anything is established or transferred.
