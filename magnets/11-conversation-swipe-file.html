The Conversation Swipe File

Almost nothing on the other sheets moves until a conversation happens — with your accountant, your HR team, your super fund, your partner. The hardest part is usually the first sentence. Here are seven of them, ready to send.

How to use these. Turn a card over to see what is in it, then take the full text below. Replace anything in green with your own detail. None of them asks anyone for advice you would have to pay for — each one asks a specific question that has a factual answer.

The full text

Copy any of these straight out. They are deliberately short — a long message gets a slow reply.

The one rule that makes these work

Every message asks a closed question with a factual answer — a figure, a yes or no, a document. "What should I do about my super?" invites a meeting that never gets booked. "What is my concessional headroom this financial year?" gets a number back the same week. Keep the ask narrow and the reply comes fast.

General information only. These are templates for asking questions, not advice about what to do with the answers. Nothing here recommends a product, strategy or structure, and none of it considers your objectives, financial situation or needs. Whether any action is appropriate for you depends on your circumstances — seek personal advice before acting on anything you learn.
