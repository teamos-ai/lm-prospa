The Next Dollar Decision Map

A bonus lands. A loan is repaid. Income steps up. The question is always the same — mortgage, super, or investments outside super? There is no universal answer, but there is a reliable order of questions. Work down it until one stops you.

Why this order. Each question below either removes a destination or changes what the others are worth. Answering them out of order is how people end up with a technically reasonable decision that does not suit them.

Six questions, in order

Each one is a fork. Some are hard stops.

01

Is there any high-interest or non-deductible consumer debt?

Credit cards, personal loans, buy-now-pay-later balances. Nothing else competes with a guaranteed, after-tax return equal to the interest rate you are paying.

If yes — stop here. This is the only question on the page with a near-universal answer. Clear it first.
02

Could you survive six to twelve months without income?

Liquidity is not a return, it is permission. Without it, the first unexpected event forces you to sell a long-term asset at whatever price the market offers that week.

If no — stop here. Build the reserve before committing capital to anything with a timeframe attached.
If yes — continue. The remaining questions are about efficiency rather than safety.
03

When do you need this money back?

This single answer eliminates more options than any other. Money needed inside three years does not belong anywhere it can fall, and money you want before 60 does not belong inside superannuation.

Within 0–3 years — the question is cash, offset or a term deposit. Growth assets introduce a risk the timeframe cannot absorb.
Before preservation age — superannuation is out, however attractive the tax treatment. See the Gap Years Map.
After preservation age — every destination stays on the table. Continue.
04

What is your marginal tax rate, and does Division 293 apply to you?

The gap between your marginal rate and the rate inside super is the entire case for a concessional contribution. That gap narrows sharply once Division 293 applies, which changes — but does not necessarily remove — the argument.

The arithmetic — see the table below. The wider the gap, the more a dollar is worth inside super rather than outside it.
05

Is your mortgage deductible or non-deductible?

Repaying non-deductible debt produces a guaranteed, tax-free return equal to your loan rate. Repaying deductible debt does not — the interest was reducing your taxable income, so the real return is lower than the headline rate suggests.

Non-deductible (your home) — a certain return with no volatility. It competes seriously with growth assets, and wins outright for anyone who values certainty.
Deductible (investment) — compare the after-tax cost of the debt, not the rate on the statement.
06

Whose name, and in what structure?

The last question, and the one most often skipped. Ownership is decided once and lived with for decades; unwinding it later can trigger the very tax it was meant to manage.

Before you transfer — personal name, spouse, joint, trust, company, bond and super each behave differently on income, on sale and on death. See Wealth Structures, Compared.

The gap that drives question four

Even at the narrowest, a 17-point gap is not nothing — it is the same gap that makes super worth considering for someone on $135,000. The point of question four is not whether super wins. It is knowing which number you are actually comparing.

The four destinations

What each one is genuinely good at, and the thing people most often miss about it. Green marks a strength; amber marks the catch.

A

The mortgage

A guaranteed, tax-free return equal to your loan rate, with no volatility.

  • Certain. No market risk, no timing risk, no fees.
  • An offset keeps the money reachable while still saving interest.
  • Repaying directly may not be reachable again without refinancing.
  • Builds no income stream — only reduces an expense.
Best when certainty matters and the debt is non-deductible
B

Superannuation

The most tax-effective structure most people will ever have access to.

  • Contributions and earnings taxed concessionally.
  • Unused concessional cap can be carried forward, within limits.
  • Locked until preservation age — 60 for everyone reaching it from 1 July 2024.
  • Caps are annual and largely use-it-or-lose-it.
Best when the timeframe is beyond 60 and the tax gap is wide
C

Investments outside super

The only pool that can fund the years before preservation age.

  • Reachable at any age, for any purpose.
  • Ownership can be chosen to suit your tax position.
  • Earnings taxed at your marginal rate unless held elsewhere.
  • Selling to fund something else can trigger capital gains tax.
Best when you want optionality before 60
D

Cash and the reserve

Not an investment. The thing that stops you being forced to sell one.

  • Immediate, certain, no sequencing risk.
  • Funds short-term goals without touching long-term assets.
  • Loses purchasing power to inflation over long holdings.
  • Interest is taxed at your marginal rate.
Best for 0–3 year money and the emergency reserve

What the map cannot tell you

Where the balance sits between these four depends on your marginal rate, your existing structures, your debt, your timeframe, your family circumstances and how much certainty you want. This page gives you the questions in a sensible order. The weighting is personal — and that is what a conversation is for.

General information only. Rates and thresholds shown are for the 2026–27 Australian financial year and may change. Nothing here is a recommendation to repay debt, contribute to superannuation, or invest in anything.
