Agenda item 6 · Protecting what you have built

What changed in 2020 and 2021

If your cover was arranged before 2021, it may not look like what is sold today — in ways that can favour you. Knowing which side of the line your policy sits on is the first thing a reviewer will ask.

The earlier market

What older policies can still hold

Policies written under the earlier market.

  • Agreed value cover was available: the benefit was fixed when the policy was issued, regardless of what your income did afterwards.
  • Insurers expected to no longer offer agreed value contracts from 31 March 2020.
  • Older contracts generally keep their original terms while they remain in force.
From 1 July 2021

What newer cover looks like

The terms applying to newer individual cover.

  • Indemnity basis: benefits are based on your income over the preceding twelve months, assessed at claim time.
  • Benefits plus other earned income are limited to 100% of income for the first six months.
  • Beyond six months, that limit reduces to 75% of income.

Why this matters before you cancel anything. An older agreed-value policy may offer terms no longer available for purchase. It is also more expensive, and may be insuring an income you have long since exceeded. Neither fact settles the question on its own — but replacing cover is a one-way door, and the new policy is underwritten against your health today, not your health when the old one was written.

Twelve questions

Tick the ones you can answer with certainty. The ones you leave blank are the agenda.

01
What would actually be paid each month, and against which income figure?

Most cover pays a percentage of income. Which income, and when it is measured, is what decides the cheque.

02
Is my cover agreed value or indemnity?

It decides whether the benefit was fixed when the policy was issued, or will be worked out from your recent earnings at claim time.

03
How long is the waiting period, and who funds it?

Thirty, sixty or ninety days with no benefit. Something has to carry that gap — a cash reserve, leave entitlements, or selling an asset.

04
How long does the benefit last — two years, five, or to 65?

A two-year benefit and a to-65 benefit are different products at different prices. Plenty of people hold the first while assuming the second.

05
Is my cover inside super, outside it, or both?

Inside super the premium comes from the balance rather than cash flow. Definitions can be narrower, and a benefit must satisfy a condition of release before it is paid out.

06
If I changed employer or fund tomorrow, what happens to this cover?

Cover arranged through an employer or a default fund belongs to that arrangement rather than to you. Whether it follows you, and on what terms, is a question for the policy — not an assumption.

07
Does it cover bonuses and incentives, or only base salary?

For a senior executive the variable part can be a large share of total earnings. It is also the part most often left outside the definition.

08
Is my TPD cover "own occupation" or "any occupation"?

The difference is whether you must be unable to do your job, or unable to do almost any job. That is the difference between a claim paid and a claim declined.

09
Are my premiums stepped or level?

Stepped premiums rise each year with age. Level premiums start higher and rise more slowly. Which is cheaper depends entirely on how long you hold the policy.

10
Would my life cover clear the debts and support the family, not just repay the mortgage?

Clearing the loan and replacing two decades of income are very different sums.

11
When was this last reviewed against my income and debt today?

Cover arranged against an earlier salary rarely matches a later one. The gap widens quietly with every promotion.

12
Who receives a benefit, and would it reach them without delay?

Ownership, nomination and structure decide where the money lands and how fast. See The Estate Checklist.

Before you change anything

A one-way door

Review before you replace. Never cancel existing cover until replacement cover has been issued and accepted — underwriting is assessed against your health now, and a condition that has appeared since the original policy was written can change what is available to you, or make it unavailable entirely.
