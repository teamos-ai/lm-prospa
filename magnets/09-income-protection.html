The Income Protection Audit

Everything on your balance sheet was bought with income you have not earned yet. While you are still accumulating, your future earnings are the largest asset you own — and the one most likely to be insured against the wrong figure, under terms agreed years ago.

What changed, and why old policies are different

If your cover was arranged before 2021, it may not resemble what is available now — in ways that can favour you.

Before the changes

Policies written under the earlier market.

  • Agreed value cover was available: the benefit was fixed when the policy was issued, regardless of what your income did afterwards.
  • Insurers expected to no longer offer agreed value contracts from 31 March 2020.
  • Older contracts generally keep their original terms while they remain in force.

From 1 July 2021

The terms applying to newer individual cover.

  • Indemnity basis: benefits are based on your income over the preceding twelve months, assessed at claim time.
  • Benefits plus other earned income are limited to 100% of income for the first six months.
  • Beyond six months, that limit reduces to 75% of income.

Why this matters before you cancel anything. An older agreed-value policy may offer terms no longer available for purchase. It is also more expensive, and may be insuring an income you have long since exceeded. Neither fact settles the question on its own — but replacing cover is a one-way door, and the new policy is underwritten against your health today, not your health when the old one was written.

Eleven questions

Tick the ones you can answer with certainty. The ones you leave blank are the agenda.

01
What monthly benefit would actually be paid, and against which income figure?

Most cover pays a percentage of income. The figure it is measured against — and when it is measured — is what decides the cheque.

02
Is my cover agreed value or indemnity?

It determines whether the benefit was fixed at the start or will be assessed against your recent earnings at claim time.

03
How long is the waiting period, and who funds it?

Thirty, sixty or ninety days of no benefit. That gap is funded by your reserve, your leave entitlements, or by selling something.

04
How long is the benefit period — two years, five, or to 65?

A two-year benefit period and a to-65 benefit period are different products at different prices. Many people hold the first while assuming the second.

05
Is my cover inside super, outside it, or both?

Inside super the premium comes from the balance rather than cash flow, but definitions can be narrower and benefits must satisfy a condition of release to be paid out.

06
Does my income protection cover bonuses and incentives, or only base salary?

For a senior executive, the variable component can be a large share of total earnings — and the part most often excluded from the definition.

07
Are my TPD definitions "own occupation" or "any occupation"?

The difference is whether you must be unable to do your job, or unable to do almost any job. It is the difference between a claim paid and a claim declined.

08
Are my premiums stepped or level?

Stepped premiums rise each year with age; level premiums start higher and rise more slowly. Which is cheaper depends entirely on how long you hold the policy.

09
Would my life cover clear the debts and support the family, not just repay the mortgage?

Clearing the loan and replacing two decades of income are very different sums.

10
When was this last reviewed against my current income and debt?

Cover arranged against an earlier salary rarely matches a later one. The gap widens quietly, every year you are promoted.

11
Who receives any benefit paid, and would it reach them without delay?

Ownership, nomination and structure decide where the money lands and how fast. See the Estate & Beneficiary Checklist.

The sequence that matters

Review before you replace. Never cancel existing cover until replacement cover has been issued and accepted — underwriting is assessed against your health now, and a condition that has appeared since the original policy was written can change what is available to you, or make it unavailable entirely.
